The risk

The danger doesn’t look dangerous.

It looks like a helpful chatbot, a faster prototype, an agent that saves a few hours. Here’s what it looks like from the inside.

The quiet leak

Secrets walk out one prompt at a time.

An employee pastes your pricing model into a chatbot to tidy it up. Now it sits on someone else’s servers, under someone else’s rules.

The copycat

Your edge becomes someone else’s.

Designs, playbooks, and client lists end up in data you don’t control, where they can help a competitor, or someone worse.

The exposed build

New products, built on borrowed trust.

Your team builds on public AI, with passwords in the code and customer data in every request.

The runaway machine

Agents acting with no one watching.

AI agents get access to email, files, and payments, then act with no one checking and no record of what they did.

Behind all of it

The question everyone is asking: who is really in control of AI? It should be you.

What’s at stake

Slowing down isn’t safety. It’s how you lose.

Lock it down

Your people use it anyway, on personal accounts you can’t see. Competitors move ahead while you wait.

Ignore it, and the leaks add up.

Your secrets leave one helpful prompt at a time. You find out from a client, a regulator, or a headline.
